Overview

The Highlands Loop

Four days from Glasgow through Edinburgh, across the western Highlands to the Isle of Skye, and home down the west side — Glencoe, Kilchurn Castle on Loch Awe, Loch Lomond and the M74 to Larkhall. As much as possible, without it turning into a checklist race.

The camper — a 2018 Volkswagen California — is collected in Glasgow on Wednesday at 14:00. A short, high-impact walk through Edinburgh's Old Town comes first, then the Trossachs, Bridge of Orchy, Glencoe, Glenfinnan and the A87 to the Skye Bridge.

Friday runs Trotternish and then crosses to Neist Point. On Saturday the route turns west at Invergarry, not north to Loch Ness: down the A82 through Fort William and Glencoe to Tyndrum, west on the A85 to Kilchurn Castle on Loch Awe, then back down Loch Lomond to the M74 at Larkhall.

Before you park

Can you park a campervan anywhere in Scotland?

No.

Scotland's famous "right to roam" does not mean campervans can park or camp anywhere.

Scottish outdoor access rights generally apply to responsible non-motorised recreation. They do not give motor vehicles the right to drive onto or stay on private land.

Park, don't camp

When using simple overnight parking, behave as if you are parking rather than establishing a full campsite. Keep chairs, tables, awnings and other equipment inside unless explicitly allowed.

Scottish Outdoor Access Code

Do

  • Use campsites
  • Use official motorhome stopovers
  • Use legal overnight parking
  • Use Forestry and Land Scotland Stay the Night locations
  • Respect local signs
  • Use designated campervan permit areas
  • Ask landowner permission where appropriate

Do not

  • Park in passing places
  • Block gates
  • Drive onto grassland
  • Empty grey water into nature
  • Dump chemical toilet waste
  • Leave rubbish
  • Create a campsite on roadside parking areas

Isle of Skye

Driving single-track roads

Many roads on Skye are narrow single-track roads with passing places. The passing place is part of the road system, not a viewpoint or a parking bay.

  • Never park in passing places.
  • Use passing places to let vehicles pass.
  • Let faster vehicles behind you pass.
  • Be cautious around blind corners.
  • Expect sheep on the road.
  • Drive slower than the posted maximum when necessary.

Start in Glasgow. Walk through historic Edinburgh. Cross the western Highlands. Drive the A87 over the Skye Bridge. Explore the dramatic north of the island, then the far west at Neist Point. Leave Skye by Glen Shiel and turn south for Glencoe. Then turn east and cross a completely different part of the Highlands before finishing at Larkhall, south-east of Glasgow. Avoid retracing the same roads whenever practical.
